Tennessee corrections commissioner resigns following failed execution of Christa Pike

Governor Bill Lee halted all executions in Tennessee for the year after inmate Christa Pike survived two lethal injections.

Tennessee Department of Correction Commissioner Frank Strada resigned on Saturday after the failed execution of death row inmate Christa Pike. Governor Bill Lee announced the departure in a joint statement with Strada. Lee halted all executions in the state for the rest of the year and ordered an independent review of the incident.

Pike survived two lethal doses of pentobarbital on Wednesday. An ambulance transported her to a hospital in Nashville, where doctors attempted to clear the drugs from her body. Her lawyers stated in legal filings that she was unconscious and on a ventilator as of Thursday night.

Strada said his resignation is "in the best interests" of the state. He called the investigation into the execution "entirely appropriate and necessary". The Department of Correction defended its protocol and said rules permitted no procedures beyond those carried out. Pike's lawyers called Strada's resignation "justified", and said the decision provided no immediate help to their client.

Pike is the only woman on death row in Tennessee. In 1995, at age 18, she and Tadaryl Shipp killed 19-year-old Colleen Slemmer in Knoxville. A Tennessee court sentenced Pike to death in 1996. Before Wednesday's execution, the US Supreme Court rejected two separate efforts to stop the lethal injection, and Lee denied a clemency request.

Pike's lawyers had previously warned that her small veins and thrombocytosis, a blood-clotting condition, created a risk of unnecessary pain during a lethal injection. Her attorneys filed an emergency motion on Friday to preserve "any and all evidence" from the execution. State officials have offered no explanation for what went wrong, and authorities have not said whether they will try again to execute Pike.

  • Tennessee Department of Correction Commissioner Frank Strada resigned on Saturday, 3 October 2026.
  • Christa Pike, the only woman on death row in Tennessee, survived two lethal doses of pentobarbital on Wednesday.
  • Governor Bill Lee ordered an independent review into the execution and halted all executions in the state for the remainder of the year.

Learn · Physics from first principles

Motion is the change in position over time

Reference frames and dynamic variables allow observers to calculate how physical bodies move through space.

A person walking across a room is an everyday example of locomotion. External body parts move, and the person changes position relative to the floor. In physics, motion is the change in position of an object or fluid with respect to a reference frame over a given time. An object with a constant position relative to its surroundings is at rest or stationary. Modern physics holds that there is no absolute frame of reference, so Isaac Newton's concept of absolute motion cannot be determined. Everything in the universe can be considered to be in motion.

Describing motion mathematically requires vector quantities. Displacement combines distance and direction. Velocity combines speed and direction. When an observer watches an object, relative motion is the object's motion described in the observer's comoving frame. Observers quantify this relationship through relative position and relative velocity. Physics divides the study of motion into two branches. Kinematics describes the motion of objects without reference to their cause. Dynamics studies forces and their effect on motion.

Physicists describe physical systems through equations of motion. These equations express the behavior of a system as a function of time. Mathematical functions track dynamic variables. In classical mechanics, functions exist in a Euclidean space. The dynamic variables are spatial coordinates and time, and can include momentum components. When the dynamics of a system is known, the equations of motion provide solutions for the differential equations.

A concrete example shows how these equations work in practice. Classical mechanics uses two dynamic variables, spatial coordinates and time, to calculate the state of an object. For a macroscopic body such as a spacecraft, Sir Isaac Newton and Leonhard Euler formulated three laws of mechanics. Compiled on July 5, 1687, these three laws allow an observer to calculate two exact values: location and velocity. At the atomic scale, the calculation changes. For subatomic particles such as electrons, the Heisenberg uncertainty principle prevents location and velocity from being determined at the same time.

Humans perceive everyday locomotion, but struggle to sense motion on larger scales. This imperceptible motion occurs for two reasons. Newton's third law of motion prevents the feeling of motion on a mass to which the observer is connected. In addition, the lack of an obvious reference frame prevents individuals from seeing that they are moving. Kinematics describes how objects move across space and time. Dynamics examines forces and their causes. What forces act on a body to produce its motion?

Key facts

  • July 5, 1687: Sir Isaac Newton published Philosophiæ Naturalis Principia Mathematica, compiling three laws of motion.
  • Three laws: Sir Isaac Newton and Leonhard Euler formulated three laws of classical mechanics.
  • Two frameworks: Classical mechanics describes super-atomic objects, and quantum mechanics describes atomic and subatomic particles.

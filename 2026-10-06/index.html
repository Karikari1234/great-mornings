Three scientists win Nobel medicine prize for research on brain cells

Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel share the award for work on optogenetics.

The Nobel Prize in medicine was awarded Monday to three scientists who studied how the brain switches the activity of individual nerve cells on or off. Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel conducted research into light-gated ion channels and optogenetics.

The Nobel committee wrote on X that the method "is now being used in laboratories around the world to reveal the brain's mysteries." Thomas Perlmann, secretary of the medicine committee, reached all three winners by phone on Monday.

Deisseroth, 54, is based at Stanford University in California. Hegemann, 71, is at Humboldt University in Berlin, and Nagel, 73, is at the University of Würzburg in Germany.

The prize money this year is 12 million Swedish kronor, or about $1.2 million. Last year, the prize went to three scientists who researched the immune system.

The announcement at the Karolinska Institute in Stockholm opened Nobels week. The physics prize follows on Tuesday, chemistry on Wednesday, and literature on Thursday. The peace prize is awarded on Friday, followed by the Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences on Oct. 12.

Key facts

  • 12 million Swedish kronor (about $1.2 million)
  • Karl Deisseroth, Peter Hegemann and Georg Nagel
  • Oct. 5, 2026

Bangladesh remittance inflows rise 15% to $8.8 billion in FY27

Remittance inflows reached $8.798 billion between July 1 and October 3 as expatriates sent more money home.

Bangladesh received $8.798 billion in remittances between July 1 and October 3 in the current fiscal year, FY27. The total is a 15.1% increase compared to the $7.643 billion received during the same period of the previous fiscal year.

Arief Hossain Khan, executive spokesperson of Bangladesh Bank, confirmed the data on Sunday, October 4. Khan noted that the growth in remittances was driven by an increase in funds sent home by Bangladeshi expatriate workers during the first few months of the fiscal year.

During the first 20 days of September, remittances worth $1.877 billion arrived in the country, according to an updated report from the central bank. Expatriates sent $130 million across three days from September 17 to 19. In the first 19 days of September last year, remittance receipts were $1.869 billion.

Gross foreign exchange reserves were approximately $36.14 billion as of September 29, according to Bangladesh Bank data. Earlier on September 20, total foreign exchange reserves were at $36.30 billion. Under the International Monetary Fund's BPM6 calculation method at that time, reserves were $31.45 billion.

British forces capture Manila from Spain in Seven Years' War

The Battle of Manila in 1762 ended in British victory over Spain and began a twenty-month occupation.

The Battle of Manila concluded in 1762 with a British victory over Spain. The victory brought a twenty-month occupation. Spain had entered the Seven Years' War that year as an ally of France. After an unsuccessful invasion of Portugal, Spanish forces lost both Havana and Manila to Great Britain.

The fighting in Manila was part of a global war that lasted from 1756 to 1763. The primary warring states were Great Britain and Prussia against France and Austria. Other powers joined the coalitions. Portugal entered alongside Great Britain, while Spain and Russia aligned with France and Austria.

The conflict followed the War of the Austrian Succession, which lasted from 1740 to 1748. That earlier war ended with the Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle, though none of the signatories were happy with the terms. Empress Maria Theresa of Austria signed the treaty to gain time to rebuild her military forces. In 1756, France and Austria signed the Treaty of Versailles and declared war on Britain. A second agreement in 1757 brought Prussia into the war on the side of Britain.

In North America, clashes began before the formal declarations in Europe. French forces built a chain of forts in the Ohio River Valley during the early 1750s to protect claims to the Mississippi River basin. On 28 May 1754, George Washington led Virginia colonial militia and Mingo warriors in an ambush at Jumonville Glen. Ten French soldiers died in the attack, along with commander Joseph Coulon de Jumonville. French forces retaliated on 3 July 1754 at Fort Necessity and forced Washington to surrender. In July 1755, British Major General Edward Braddock led about 2,000 troops on an expedition to retake Fort Duquesne, which ended in defeat.

The Seven Years' War ended in 1763 through two peace settlements. The Treaty of Hubertusburg confirmed Prussian occupation of Silesia. Under the Treaty of Paris, Great Britain returned Manila and Havana to Spain. France lost its possessions in North America. Britain established commercial dominance in India.

Learn · Physics from first principles

Energy is the conserved capacity to do work

The total energy of an isolated system remains constant across all forms.

Living organisms constantly take in and release energy, and radiant energy from the Sun drives the Earth's climate and ecosystems. Energy is the quantitative property transferred to a body or to a physical system, recognizable in the capacity to do work and in the form of heat and light. In the International System of Units, the unit of measurement for energy is the joule.

The total energy of a system is classified into kinetic energy and potential energy. Kinetic energy is determined by the movement of an object or the composite motion of its components. Potential energy is based upon the position of an object within a field. Combinations of potential and kinetic energy form distinct categories, such as mechanical energy.

A calculation of kinetic energy uses the formulation proposed by Gottfried Leibniz in the late 17th century. Leibniz defined vis viva as the product of the mass of an object and its velocity squared. Kinetic energy differs from vis viva by a factor of two. To calculate the kinetic energy of an object, one multiplies its mass by its velocity squared, and divides that product by two.

Energy is a conserved quantity. The law of conservation of energy states that the total energy of an isolated system remains constant as it converts between forms. In the early 18th century, Émilie du Châtelet proposed the conservation of energy in the marginalia of her translation of Newton's Principia Mathematica. In 1845, James Prescott Joule discovered the link between mechanical work and the generation of heat. Since 1918, theorists have understood from Noether's theorem that the conservation of energy is the direct mathematical consequence of the translational symmetry of time.

In 1905, Albert Einstein showed through special relativity that rest mass corresponds to an equivalent amount of rest energy. Rest mass converts to equivalent amounts of kinetic energy and electromagnetic radiant energy. All forms of energy contribute to total mass and total energy. Conservation of energy and conservation of mass are one equivalent law.

Energy also takes discrete values at atomic scales. In the early 1800s, Joseph von Fraunhofer and William Hyde Wollaston observed spectral lines in light from the sun. In 1913, Danish physicist Niels Bohr proposed the notion of quantized energy levels in the Bohr theory of the atom. How did Erwin Schrödinger and Werner Heisenberg explain these energy levels in terms of the Schrödinger equation?

Key facts

  • In 1845, James Prescott Joule discovered the link between mechanical work and the generation of heat.
  • Albert Einstein showed in 1905 that rest mass corresponds to an equivalent amount of rest energy.
  • In 1913, Niels Bohr proposed the notion of quantized energy levels in atoms.

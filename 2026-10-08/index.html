Cover story

Three killed as Houthi missiles strike two Saudi Arabian airports

The strikes on Riyadh and Abha follow a collapse of a fragile truce in Yemen and a fight for a global shipping route.

Picture the arrival halls of King Khalid International Airport in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia's capital, and Abha airport near the southern border. Over two days in October, missiles and drones hit these spaces. Two women, from Morocco and Algeria, died in Abha. A Sudanese man died in Riyadh. Thirty-six others were wounded.

The Houthis, a rebel group that controls north-west Yemen with backing from Iran, claimed the attacks. They used drones and ballistic missiles. A ballistic missile flies like a thrown stone. It is powered at first and then falls heavily in an arc toward its target. Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree said the strikes answered 60 airstrikes by a Saudi-led coalition. That coalition backs Yemen's recognised government. The Houthis want to hold and expand their territory. The Saudi coalition wants to restore the official government.

Violence recently spiked along the Red Sea coast. Houthi fighters took the port of Mokha on the Bab al-Mandab Strait. This narrow waterway is a chokepoint for global shipping. If this strait closes, cargo ships bound for Asia and Bangladesh must take longer routes around Africa. Saudi-backed forces claim they have secured the strait and declared all-out war to take territory back.

The Saudi coalition responded to the airport attacks quickly. They struck more than 80 Houthi military sites across four Yemeni provinces. A ceasefire paused the conflict in 2022, but the fighting has now resumed. The United Nations says the new violence has displaced 200,000 people.

The airport attacks expose a brittle system where a fight over a local strait can disrupt global trade and lock neighbours into long conflicts. The Saudi coalition has declared an all-out war to retake territory from the Houthis.

Key facts

  • Three people were killed and 36 wounded across two Saudi airports on October 6 and 7.
  • Houthi rebels fired ballistic missiles and drones at the airports.
  • The United Nations says 200,000 people have been displaced by the recent escalation in fighting.

US trade shifts as Bangladesh buys more energy and sells fewer garments

Garment exports to the United States are falling while purchases of American fuel and raw materials surge.

Imagine a busy port where ships unload American fuel and raw materials while fewer containers of locally made shirts leave for the United States. Trade between Bangladesh and the United States is shifting. The United States buys less clothing, and Bangladesh buys more American petroleum and cotton.

Between January and June of 2026, Bangladesh imported goods worth $1.8962 billion from the United States. Purchases accelerated quickly. Imports jumped 113.11 percent from $605.6 million in the first three months of the year to $1.2906 billion in the spring. Petroleum products made up the bulk of this increase. Energy imports rose from $135.5 million early in the year to $775.2 million between April and June.

The recent Agreement on Reciprocal Trade commits Bangladesh to buy more energy and agricultural commodities from the United States. A bilateral trade agreement is a binding contract between two nations. It sets formal rules for commerce and obligates countries to meet specific purchase targets.

Meanwhile, garment exports are losing ground. Data from the US Office of Textiles and Apparel shows American clothing imports from Bangladesh fell 4.43 percent to $5.39 billion between January and August. The total volume dropped, and the average price per unit also fell.

More purchases are planned. Biman Bangladesh Airlines has agreements to buy 25 Boeing aircraft. The first 14 planes cost about $3.7 billion. These future deliveries will add billions to the country's import bill. This tests a financial system that recorded a $3.82 billion national trade deficit in July and August of 2026.

Learn · Physics from first principles

Physics from first principles, lesson 7: waves

Waves are dynamic disturbances that propagate through physical media or empty space via neighbor-to-neighbor interactions.

At a sporting event, the height of the spectators can form a wave. One person stands up. The person next to them responds a moment later and stands up too. This neighbor-to-neighbor interaction with a delay creates a change from a resting state that moves across the space.

In physics, a wave is a disturbance in a field resulting from a delayed response to adjacent disturbances. A local deformation in a medium propagates from particle to particle. It creates local stresses that cause strain in neighboring particles. The effect moves at a finite speed.

Classical physics studies two main types of waves. Mechanical waves travel through a physical medium like water or solid rock. Electromagnetic waves involve electric and magnetic fields. They can travel through a vacuum. Examples of electromagnetic waves include radio waves and visible light.

Waves are classified by the direction of their physical disturbance. In a longitudinal wave, the movement aligns with the direction of travel. Sound waves in air are always longitudinal. In a transverse wave, the disturbance is perpendicular to the direction of travel. Electromagnetic waves are strictly transverse.

When a wave travels in one direction, it is a traveling wave. A pair of identical waves moving in opposite directions makes a standing wave. In a standing wave, the vibration has spots where the amplitude is zero. Waves of the same type often meet at a given point in space and time.

When waves overlap, the properties at that point are the sum of the properties of each component wave. Real ocean surfaces consist of many overlapping waves with different amplitudes and directions. How do we describe the distribution of energy across these complex, overlapping waves?

Key facts

  • Mechanical waves propagate through physical media like water or solid rock.
  • Electromagnetic waves can travel through a vacuum.
  • Sound waves in fluids like air are always longitudinal.

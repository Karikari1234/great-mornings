Trump will talk to Putin in China while his envoys talk Ukraine in Berlin

On Thursday the US plans two meetings on the Ukraine war: Trump with Putin in China, and US envoys with Ukrainians and Europeans in Berlin.

On Thursday, the United States is set to be in two rooms at once. In China, President Donald Trump is due to meet Russian President Vladimir Putin one-on-one. In Berlin, American envoys are due to meet Ukrainian officials.

That comes from a US official who told Al Jazeera about the plans on Wednesday. The official called the two tracks "a coordinated diplomatic effort."

So who is going to Berlin? Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, the envoys, who are expected to sit down with Ukrainian officials and European partners. Their job is to develop post-war security guarantees and a comprehensive economic recovery plan for Kyiv.

A security guarantee is simply a promise about how a country will be protected once the fighting stops. A recovery plan is about how it gets rebuilt. Both are about the day after the war, which is odd to plan for while the war goes on.

Now the China side. Trump and Putin last met in Alaska in August 2025 for negotiations to end the war. They failed to reach a settlement.

Here's the catch: the one-on-one is not the only meeting. Trump and Putin are also scheduled to join Chinese President Xi Jinping for a three-way summit on the sidelines of the APEC forum in Shenzhen, a regular gathering of Asia-Pacific economies.

Al Jazeera calls it the first-ever gathering of the leaders of the world's three major powers. Trump and Putin were already photographed shaking hands, with Xi looking on, at an APEC reception in Shenzhen on November 18.

The US official put the aim this way: "President Trump remains committed to ending the fighting and the tremendous human suffering, while helping establish the conditions for a sovereign, secure and prosperous Ukraine."

Alaska ended without a settlement. The report does not say what Trump and Putin will discuss in Shenzhen, so whether this meeting goes any differently is the open question.

Key facts

  • Trump is set to meet Putin one-on-one in China on Thursday, a US official told Al Jazeera.
  • Witkoff and Kushner are expected to meet Ukrainian officials and European partners in Berlin the same day.
  • Trump and Putin last met in Alaska in August 2025 and reached no settlement.
  • Trump, Putin and Xi are also scheduled for a trilateral summit at APEC in Shenzhen.

Hasina wants a passport home; troops deploy as violence spreads

Sheikh Hasina plans to return from exile in December and has asked for a passport, which the government refuses, while bombings and arson spread and paramilitary troops deploy nationwide.

How do you fly home when your own government won't give you a passport? That puzzle sits at the centre of Bangladesh's latest week of unrest.

Sheikh Hasina has lived in exile in India since fleeing Dhaka in August 2024. She says she plans to return in December, and she has asked for a new passport and national identity card. So far the government has said no.

Here's the detail that matters. Her lawyers emailed the request to Bangladesh's diplomatic mission in New Delhi. The government says an email isn't enough and that she has to apply in person.

The stakes for her are high. In November 2025 a tribunal sentenced her to death in absentia for crimes against humanity linked to the crackdown on the 2024 uprising. She denies wrongdoing and rejects the verdict.

She told the Associated Press: "If death is my destiny, I would rather die on the soil of my own country, among my own people."

Meanwhile the fight has moved onto the streets. Her Awami League, banned in Bangladesh, announced a week of protests and rallies ending in a nationwide lockdown on November 19.

How widespread is it? Local media cited by Reuters say police recorded dozens of crude bomb blasts and vehicle burnings in at least 28 districts over 10 days. Bangladesh has 64 districts, so that's close to half the country.

Grameen Bank says at least 37 of its branches and offices have faced arson or attempted arson since November 10, 14 of them after midnight on Tuesday.

Some of the attacks have killed people. A bus company helper was burned to death in Pirojpur on Wednesday morning after two buses were set on fire. Police say unidentified attackers allegedly killed a BNP activist during a protest near Gopalganj, Hasina's home district, on Tuesday night.

On Wednesday the government sent hundreds of paramilitary troops across the country. Home Minister Salahuddin Ahmed accused Awami League leaders abroad of inciting the unrest, and Dhaka police arrested at least 71 members of banned groups in 24 hours. Who carried out each attack hasn't been established.

Watch two dates. The first is the lockdown, set for today, November 19. The second is December, when Hasina says she will come home, though she still doesn't have a passport.

  • Hasina, in exile in India since August 2024, says she will return in December and has asked for a passport and ID card, which the government has refused.

Then he gestured at Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi. "And she may be even tougher. And we're okay, too," he said.

So what was the room? The Quad is a group of four countries: the US, India, Japan and Australia. This was its first in-person leaders' summit since 2024, held on the sidelines of the 49th ASEAN Summit.

Trump's argument was that friendship helps. "I've been with all three of them for a long time, and they're phenomenal people, and they're friends of mine, so that helps," he said. Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese was the third leader beside him.

Now the part with substance. Rare earths are minerals that go into many electronics, and a few big suppliers dominate the world's supply. Trump said the US is making rare earths at a level it has never made before, and "we're gonna catch them within 10 months."

Here's the catch. That is Trump's claim, and the source gives no production figures to check it against.

What can be checked is the schedule. The summit is expected to shift the Quad from strategic talk to commercial action. That includes an inaugural Business Leaders' Forum and a Super Intelligence Accelerator on AI and advanced computing.

Trump also said he welcomed Nvidia's move to set up a national security and export control oversight committee, calling it a good idea. He kept open a possible meeting with Russian President Vladimir Putin in China, ahead of his China visit for APEC and talks with President Xi Jinping.

And then there is the money. Export credit agencies, the government-backed lenders that help finance projects, from all four countries were due to meet in Tokyo on November 18 to work out financing for joint projects.

That meeting is the one to watch. Warm words are cheap. A financing plan with names and projects attached would show whether this friendship turns into anything you could build.

Ukraine hits Kerch Bridge again, and the strait below it is now blocked

A Ukrainian strike dropped part of the Kerch Bridge into the sea, and the wreckage may matter as much for ships as for cars and trains.

The bridge was not the only thing that got blocked. According to DW, debris from the Kerch Bridge strike now sits in the main shipping route between the Black Sea and the Sea of Azov.

Here is the setup. The Kerch Bridge is 19 kilometers long and links Russian-annexed Crimea with Russia's Krasnodar region. Putin's government began building it after annexing Crimea in 2014, and it was finished four years later.

Ukraine attacked it during the night of November 17. The highway section collapsed into the sea. Russia's transport ministry announced a "temporary suspension" of road and rail traffic, though rail services were later resumed.

Why does the sea matter? Think of the strait as a hallway. The Kerch Strait channel is only 120 meters wide and three to five meters deep, according to Mychajlo Hontschar of a Ukrainian think tank. Block a hallway that narrow and nothing gets through.

That means river-sea vessels from the Sea of Azov, the Don and the Volga cannot leave. Small ships from the Black Sea fleet and the Caspian flotilla based in Novorossiysk are stuck too.

Here's the catch. Pro-Kremlin Telegram channels say the bridge matters little for the army or for supplying Crimea. One quoted Crimean official calls it a "purely civilian structure."

Ukrainian military experts disagree. Oleksandr Kovalenko says Russia has no other land connection to replace it, and expects supply problems. His reasoning is simple: if resources run short during attacks or defense, one of them fails.

The operation itself took about a month and had seven phases, says the Ukrainian security service (SBU). First it says air defenses were neutralized, then the bridge was hit. Ukraine says it used its own drones plus FP-5 Flamingo and R-360 Neptun missiles.

Some Russian military bloggers claim Western allies helped. Ukraine has not confirmed that.

President Zelenskyy called the strike a response to Russian attacks on bridges in Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Kyiv and the Odesa region. This was Ukraine's first attack on the bridge in a year and a half, after the one in June 2025.

So the open question is whether Russia can clear that channel and keep its supply lines moving.

Key facts

  • The Kerch Bridge is 19 km long and links Crimea with Russia's Krasnodar region.
  • The highway section collapsed into the sea after the attack on the night of November 17.
  • The Kerch Strait shipping channel is about 120 meters wide and three to five meters deep.
  • Ukraine says the operation took about a month and had seven phases.

The dollar is back where it stood before "Liberation Day"

Bets on more Fed rate rises have helped lift the dollar to a 19-month high, wiping out its slump from Trump's 2025 tariff announcement.

What happens to a currency when its country's central bank keeps signalling higher interest rates? This week the dollar gave a pretty clear answer.

On November 17 the dollar index, which measures the US currency against six others including the euro, pound and yen, rose as high as 103.69. That's its highest point in 19 months, and it's back to where it was on April 2, 2025.

That date matters. It's the day President Donald Trump announced sweeping tariffs on US trading partners and called it "Liberation Day."

What came next was odd. The dollar, US government bonds and US stocks all fell together, which raised a worrying question: might foreign investors start pulling money out of America?

So how did the dollar climb all the way back? Part of the answer is interest rates. The Federal Reserve, America's central bank, sets a benchmark rate, and when it rises, holding dollar savings and bonds pays more.

Think of two savings accounts, one paying more interest than the other. Money tends to drift toward the better rate.

And the Fed is raising. It lifted rates in September for the first time in three years, then held steady in October. Minutes from that October meeting, released November 18, showed nearly all officials expected another quarter-point rise in December. Two floated half a point.

Why so keen? A "vast majority" of the Fed's 19 policymakers worried inflation could stick around even after gasoline prices fall and the effects of tariffs and AI spending fade. Governor Christopher Waller said beating inflation would take more "aggressive action."

Voyager 1 is now so far away that a text takes two days for a round trip

Voyager 1 has reached one light-day from Earth, so a message and its reply take two days.

If you send a message to Voyager 1 today, you wait two days to hear back. That is the news: the probe has reached a distance of one light-day from Earth, according to NASA.

Here's what that means. A light-day is how far light travels in 24 hours. Radio messages move at light speed, so one day to get there, and another day for the reply to come home.

The probe is about 26 billion km away. It was launched in 1977, nearly 50 years ago, and it is the most distant human-made object.

So how big is a light-day, really? NASA scientist Tiffany Kataria calls it "the best illustration of just how vast space is". It took Voyager 1 nearly 50 years to get there.

Here's the catch. A light-day is tiny by the standards of the stars. Many exoplanetary systems, Kataria noted, are at least several light-years away, and NASA expects Voyager 1 to need about 18,000 years to cover one light-year.

Still, this old probe is not finished. Voyager 1 and its twin, Voyager 2, are the only spacecraft to have reached interstellar space, the region beyond the Sun's heliosphere. Voyager 2 is expected to reach one light-day in 2035.

Some of Voyager 1's equipment, including its cameras, has been switched off remotely to save power. Think of a phone on low battery: you close the apps you can live without. The probe keeps studying invisible things like magnetic fields.

That data helps researchers study "the gas, the dust and other material that exists between stars", Kataria said.

Earlier, the Voyagers explored Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. Voyager 1's images of Jupiter first revealed that the planet has rings.

Both craft also carry a Golden Record, a 30cm gold-plated copper disc meant to tell the story of our world to extraterrestrials. After nearly 50 years of flying, the two-day wait for a reply is still the speed of the nearest neighbourhood.

Key facts

  • Voyager 1 reached one light-day from Earth, about 26 billion km away, per NASA.
  • Messages take a day to reach it and another day for a reply.
  • NASA expects it to take about 18,000 years to travel a full light-year.
  • Voyager 2 is expected to reach one light-day in 2035.

Here's the idea behind encirclement. You close a ring around an enemy army. Inside the ring, that army is cut off from the rest of its side, so retreating and getting help both get much harder.

So why Stalingrad? Germany had invaded the Soviet Union in June 1941, which opened what is called the Eastern Front. Operation Uranus came about 17 months later.

Here's the payoff. The source says Axis forces were defeated at Stalingrad in early 1943.

That defeat mattered beyond one city. After it, Allied offensives in the Pacific and the Soviet Union forced the Axis to retreat on all fronts.

Now the human scale, because it is easy to lose in a story about ring-shaped maneuvers. World War II killed 60 to 75 million people, and a majority of them were civilians. That means well over 30 million of the dead were not soldiers.

Here's the catch. Winning at Stalingrad is not the same as ending the war. After Stalingrad came the Allied landings at Normandy in 1944, the fall of Berlin, and Germany's unconditional surrender on 8 May 1945.

Japan announced its surrender on 15 August 1945 and signed the surrender document on 2 September. From Operation Uranus to that signature is nearly three years.

And even that last date is not fully settled. At the time, many took the 15 August armistice as the end of the war, while the formal surrender on 2 September is what officially ended it in Asia.

Learn · Physics from first principles

Open problems: the list of what physics cannot yet explain

The last lesson is about the questions physicists write down and keep, and why those lists matter.

Think about the last time you learned something properly. Somewhere along the way you probably found a spot where your understanding ran out, and you could name it. Physics does the same thing on a grand scale, and this final lesson is about that habit: writing down exactly what we cannot explain yet.

Physicists have been doing it for a long time. In his book Opticks, first published in 1704, Isaac Newton listed 31 queries about light and matter. These were not answers. They were questions he left standing on the page for others to chase.

Open problems come in two flavours. Some are theoretical, meaning our current theories cannot explain something we have observed. Others are experimental, meaning we do not yet know how to build a test for an idea, or how to look closely enough at a phenomenon.

Here is the biggest one. Physics has two great frameworks, the Standard Model for the tiny stuff and general relativity for gravity. Their mathematics is inconsistent with each other, and both break down in extreme places like the Big Bang and the centers of black holes. Add the nature of dark matter and dark energy, the absolute mass of neutrinos, and why there is more matter than antimatter, and you have a decent picture of the edge of the map.

Now the worked example, using lists and arithmetic. In 1900, Lord Kelvin said physics had just two clouds left: no evidence for the luminiferous aether, and the equipartition theorem failing to predict how much heat solids hold. Two problems. Within a decade, special relativity and quantum mechanics had cleared both. That is the clever bit: the small cracks turned out to be the doors to whole new physics.

Other lists have moved slower. In 1984, Barry Simon listed 15 open problems on quantum operators, and by 2000 about 5 were solved, roughly a third in 16 years. At the Strings 2000 conference, members proposed 10 millennium problems in theoretical physics, and as of 2025, none has been conclusively solved. Vitaly Ginzburg, who kept his own list from 1971 to 2005, named three great problems: the arrow of time, the interpretations of quantum mechanics, and life arising from physics alone. So here is the question to take with you, and it is the one no lesson can answer for you yet: which item on today's list is the next Kelvin cloud? For where to dig deeper, try Welch Labs, 3Blue1Brown, PBS Space Time and Veritasium.

Key facts

  • Newton's Opticks (first edition 1704) listed 31 queries about light and matter.
  • In 1900 Lord Kelvin named two clouds in classical physics; both were solved within a decade by special relativity and quantum mechanics.
  • The Standard Model is mathematically inconsistent with general relativity, and both break down at the Big Bang and inside black holes.
  • Of 10 millennium problems proposed at Strings 2000, none has been conclusively solved as of 2025.

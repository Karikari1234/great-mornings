Pentagon plans to livestream Fort Hood shooter's firing squad execution

The US military intends to publicly execute a former soldier who killed 13 people, though legal reviews will likely delay the plan.

Imagine a crowded medical building where soldiers wait in line for vaccines. The place is Fort Hood, a military base in Texas. The shooter is Nidal Hasan, a former army psychiatrist. In 2009, he shot and killed 13 unarmed soldiers there. Now, the US government wants to execute him by firing squad and broadcast the event live on the internet. US Defence Secretary Pete Hegseth says the public needs to see the serious consequences of such actions.

Hegseth and President Donald Trump want a public execution. The military scheduled it for 3 December. The legal system stands in the way. A military execution requires an appellate review. This is a process where a higher court inspects the decision to check its legality, much like a factory supervisor checking a product before it leaves the floor. John Mills, a lawyer for death row inmates, says Hasan will likely appeal to a federal court.

That legal move would likely delay the order. Eugene R Fidell, a lecturer at Yale Law School, says these court proceedings would last years. A delay would push the date far beyond Trump's current term in the White House. The broadcast itself faces severe obstacles. Large platforms like Meta and TikTok prohibit videos that show death.

Hasan admitted to the shooting in court. He said he believed he was protecting Muslims and the Taliban in Afghanistan. A military execution by firing squad is a first for the US since the end of World War Two. Last year, only Iran and Afghanistan carried out public executions. The demand for a public broadcast tests whether internet companies will override their own rules to host a state execution.

Key facts

  • Nidal Hasan killed 13 unarmed soldiers at Fort Hood in 2009.
  • The military scheduled the firing squad execution for 3 December.
  • Meta and TikTok explicitly ban livestreams of capital punishment.

If the agency approves the deal, SpaceX will compete directly against established carriers AT&T and Verizon. Following the news, shares of competing telecom companies fell more than 6 percent.

The new licenses include up to 14 megahertz of paired spectrum in the 800 MHz band. Spectrum is the invisible radio frequencies that carry data through the air. The company previously bought 2GHz spectrum from EchoStar to support its direct-to-device service. The FCC also approved SpaceX plans to deploy 15,000 second-generation Starlink Mobile satellites. SpaceX states these new satellites offer 100 times more bandwidth than current versions.

SpaceX wants to create a single network for service indoors, outdoors, and in cellular dead zones. The acquisition tests whether a company can successfully merge space-based and ground-based infrastructure into one system.

Learn · Physics from first principles

The electromagnetic spectrum and the nature of light

Visible colours are a small fraction of a vast spectrum of electromagnetic waves that travel at the speed of light.

White light passing through a prism splits into a range of colours. Isaac Newton described this in 1672. He called the range a spectrum. Newton showed these colours belong to light itself. They combine to make white light.

William Herschel expanded this idea in 1800. He moved a thermometer through the colours of split light to study their temperatures. The temperature was highest just beyond the red light. Herschel theorised the existence of invisible light, which he called calorific rays. Johann Ritter found invisible chemical rays beyond the violet end a year later.

Light was linked to electric currents and magnetic fields decades later. James Clerk Maxwell wrote equations for the electromagnetic field in the 1860s. He analysed the speed of theoretical waves in this field. Maxwell saw they travel at the known speed of light. He inferred that light is an electromagnetic wave.

Maxwell predicted an infinite range of wave frequencies that travel at the speed of light. Heinrich Hertz proved this in 1886. Hertz built a device to generate and detect low-frequency radio waves. To find their speed, Hertz measured the wavelength of the radio waves and multiplied it by their frequency. The calculation yielded the speed of light.

The spectrum organizes this radiation by frequency or wavelength. Radio waves have the lowest frequency and the longest wavelengths. These waves can span thousands of kilometers. Gamma rays sit at the high-frequency end. They have wavelengths much smaller than an atomic nucleus.

High-frequency waves carry high photon energy. Gamma rays and X-rays have enough energy to ionize atoms and cause chemical reactions. Visible light has longer wavelengths and less energy. Max Planck studied light absorption in 1901. He discovered that light is absorbed only in discrete quanta, now called photons. How does a wave act as a particle?

Key facts

  • Isaac Newton described the spectrum of colours in 1672.
  • William Herschel discovered invisible infrared radiation in 1800.
  • James Clerk Maxwell developed equations for the electromagnetic field in the 1860s.
